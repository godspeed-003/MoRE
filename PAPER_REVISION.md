## 1. The claim the paper now makes

Not "MoRE combines both benefits" — the data will not carry that. The claim is a **controlled
decomposition with a mechanism**:

> Mixture-of-Experts and Mixture-of-Recursions answer orthogonal questions — *which*
> computation, and *how much*. Their composition should inherit both. Tested at matched
> parameters from one codebase with bit-identical inputs, **it does not**: recursion alone
> (MoR) wins in-domain, adding routing (MoRE) costs in-domain quality but buys
> out-of-domain robustness, and MoRE's adaptive-depth mechanism never engages. We isolate
> why.

This is Outcome C-plus: the architecture does not deliver, **and we measured the mechanism**
rather than reporting an unexplained null. Every structural claim is either measured or
explicitly labelled a hypothesis (CLAUDE.md §4). The forward-looking scale prediction (§6)
is what turns "it failed" into "here is when it would not".

## 3. The three mechanism findings, with their evidential status

State each as measured or hypothesized (CLAUDE.md §4).

**(a) Recursion adds capacity; sparse routing divides it. [ordering MEASURED, mechanism HYPOTHESIS]**
MoR's single block sees 100% of tokens; MoE and MoRE split the FFN budget six ways on a
fixed 3-epoch budget. The ordering MoR < MoRE < MoE tracks this. The dilution account is a
*consistent hypothesis*, not an isolated effect — say so, and use it to generate the scale
prediction in §6.

**(b) Adaptive depth is real in MoR, inert in MoRE. [MEASURED — strongest single result]**
From the depth curve: MoR's learned policy (3.515) beats every uniform depth including its
own best (4.06); MoR even degrades when forced deeper. MoRE's learned policy (3.637) ties
forced-d7 (3.697) because its halting collapsed to always-max (97% forced-exit, mean depth
6.955 of 7). **This is the cleanest evidence for the paper's central contrast.**

**(c) The two mechanisms compete for one budget. [MEASURED on the dev grid; canonical-corpus confirmation pending]**
Raising the ponder weight to force adaptive depth also destroys expert differentiation
(AMI 0.29 → 0.003 on the dev grid). Depth and specialization are not independent knobs in
MoRE the way they are in MoR and MoE separately — the paper's actual novel contribution.

**And one refuted story, which is a result not a failure.** The elegant "subspace trapping"
hypothesis (route-once confines a token to one expert's subspace) is **falsified three
ways**: final-state PR is *higher* in the recursive arms, velocity decays smoothly rather
than vibrating, and updates are orthogonal rather than rescaling. Report the refutation and
the mechanism the data does support — the difference between "MoRE fails" and "MoRE fails
for this reason, which we ruled out three alternatives to reach".

## 4. §1 of the old agenda is still the largest blocking item

**Write the ACT forward path down.** The paper has never stated its own halting equations:
the per-step halt probability, the cumulative-halt/remainder recursion, the ponder cost
`N(t) + R(t)`, and — critically — **where the differentiable path to the halt head runs**
(CLAUDE.md §2 requires a real gradient there; T-LX.14 measured the halt gradient at 285×
task-dominated, which is the quantitative form of finding (b)). This is a methods section
that depends on no run. Do it first.

Also correct the `c_t + p_t > 1 − ε` notation and cite Graves 2016 and Banino et al.
(PonderNet, NeurIPS 2021) — PonderNet is also the name of the principled fix the paper's
discussion should point to (see `POST_ACL_TODO.md` §1).

## 5. Precise framing rules for this paper

- **Two tiers, never mixed.** Canonical table vs exploratory evals. Never put an
  exploratory number in the headline table.
- **Every exploratory comparison is `at the resolution floor`.** The 5v5 exact test cannot
  return p < 0.00794; every headline gap sits there. Phrase as "as extreme as a five-seed
  design admits" — weaker than "highly significant".
- **Best/last are identical** on all 15 cells (`log_interval = 1`, monotone). No model
  selection occurred. Say so; it pre-empts a reviewer question.
- **MoRE is `route-once` as defined** (`routing_persistence = "per_token"`,
  `router_calls_per_dispatch = 1/7`). The retired per-step cells in
  `archive/pre_finalization/more_l7_1_per_step/` are a labelled ablation; their
  `resolved_config.json` must never be rewritten.
- **The honesty payload** — disclose all of it (each is described in `PAPER_GUIDE.md` §7):
  fixing route-once lowered the headline by 0.134 nats; the pre-registration is partial and
  says so; ten cells carry `gpu_name = None` (do not retrofit); three loss terms are
  structurally zero on language and the `family_cls = 0` one *strengthens* the
  specialization claims.
- **Forbidden sentences** — run the checklist in `PAPER_GUIDE.md` §12 over the draft.

## 6. Limitations, written as claims

Lead with scale, because a strong reviewer will and it is correct: 5.6M params, one block,
3 epochs. Convert it from a confound into the paper's forward claim — the dilution account
in §3(a) **predicts a scale threshold** (enough tokens per expert per parameter that the
division stops dominating the multiplication). State the prediction, show all three arms
sit below it, and name the measurement that would falsify it.

Also state plainly: one corpus; 3 epochs with a demonstrably budget-dependent mechanism (so
10-epoch behaviour is genuinely unknown); one hyperparameter config per arm, frozen from a
dev-corpus grid that §3(b)/§3(c) show mis-transferred at least one weight; n = 5, putting
every headline comparison on the resolution floor.
