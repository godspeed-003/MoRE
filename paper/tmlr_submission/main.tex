\begin{abstract}
Conditional computation chooses either \emph{which} parameters process a token
---sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE)---or \emph{how much} computation it receives
---adaptive-depth weight-tied recursion (MoR). Composing them should inherit
both. We test whether it pays: one engine, three
configurations, parameters matched to 0.069\% ($\approx$5.58M), bit-identical
inputs, five seeds, exact randomisation tests under a pre-registered correction,
on WikiText-103.

All three arms lie on the quality/compute frontier, and the composition (MoRE)
buys the most quality per unit of compute. It improves on MoE by $7.9\%$
validation loss at $2.19\times$ MoE's inference FLOPs, where recursion alone needs
$9.07\times$: $72\%$ of recursion's quality gain for $24\%$ of its compute, or
$3\times$ the quality per FLOPs-multiplier. Out of domain it is stronger still---it
\emph{dominates} recursion alone, reaching lower loss at a quarter of the compute.

It is not lowest-loss in domain. Recursion alone leads by $0.1216$ nats,
significant at our resolution floor, and adding routing improves neither
in-domain quality nor routing quality over MoE; we keep the quality and compute
axes separate. We trace the deficit to a competition for one per-token budget,
excluding two rival explanations. Most transferably: a mean step count and an exit histogram cannot
establish input-dependent depth, since a uniform policy forced to the learned mean
reproduces both. Scoring against that control separates our recursive arms by a
factor of 24.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Broader Impact Statement}

This work trains 5.6M-parameter language models on WikiText-103, a public corpus
derived from Wikipedia, and evaluates them on public benchmarks. No human
subjects, annotation, or personally identifying data are involved, and the models
are far below the scale at which generative misuse is a practical concern. The
realistic effect of the work runs through other researchers' decisions rather
than through deployment: we report a negative result about an architecture
combination, and a diagnostic---scoring any learned depth policy against a
depth-matched uniform policy before describing it as adaptive---that should save
compute otherwise spent on a mechanism that is not paying for itself. The
asymmetric risk is the one worth naming: a null obtained at 5.6M parameters could
discourage work that would succeed at scale, which is why
\S\ref{sec:limitations} states the scale objection as a falsifiable prediction
with the measurement that would overturn it rather than as a hedge. The corpus
inherits the demographic and topical biases of Wikipedia, which we did not
measure and which bear on any downstream use of these checkpoints.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}

\textbf{What was run.} Fifteen runs: three architectures $\times$ five seeds
$\{42,43,44,45,46\}$, every result reported as mean $\pm$ std over the five. No
arm was tuned for itself; one optimiser configuration was fixed before the
comparison and shared by all three.

\textbf{Data.} WikiText-103 with a byte-pair vocabulary of 8{,}192 and packed
sequences of length 256, under the frozen identifier
\texttt{lang-wikitext-103-bpe8192-len256-800d6154}. Evaluation uses the corpus's
\emph{own} author-provided validation split rather than a carve-out of the
training material, which is what makes the leakage audit meaningful: no
validation or test token was ever visible to the trainer. Out-of-domain
evaluation uses held-out blocks of the Pile \citep{gao2020pile}.

\textbf{Model and optimiser.} $d_{\text{model}} = 256$, one shared block, four
attention heads, maximum recursion depth $T = 7$, batch size 48, three epochs of
AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, gradient clipping 1.0,
dropout 0.1. Parameter counts are 5{,}584{,}908 for MoE and MoRE---identical---and
5{,}581{,}063 for MoR, matched to 0.069\%, the residual being the router MoR does
not have.

\textbf{Compute.} Three epochs is $\approx$404M tokens per run and is a
\emph{fixed budget rather than convergence} (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). Training ran
on single consumer laptop GPUs; each recursive cell took 5.7--7.9 hours on an
RTX 4060 (8\,GB), so the full matrix is on the order of 60 GPU-hours. Two arms
were retrained on that card after the original checkpoints were lost and the
third remains on its original V100, which the disclosures in
\S\ref{sec:limitations} quantify: both retrained arms reproduced their published
means to $-0.117\sigma$ and $-0.119\sigma$, a common-mode offset that cancels in
the between-arm gaps carrying every verdict.

\textbf{Protocol and statistics.} The primary metric is final-epoch validation
task loss in nats; \texttt{best} equals \texttt{last} on all fifteen cells, so no
model selection occurred on either split. Comparisons use an exact two-sided
randomization test over all $\binom{10}{5} = 252$ regroupings---exhaustive, hence
deterministic, with no sampling error and no normality assumption---corrected by
Holm--Bonferroni over two families declared before the final arm was trained. The
design's resolution floor is $2/252 = 0.00794$ and every headline comparison sits
on it. Everything outside those two families is labelled exploratory and
uncorrected, and carries no significance verdict in the abstract or conclusions.

\textbf{Provenance, stated as it is.} The matrix spans four code revisions, all
with uncommitted working-tree changes at run time; comparability rests on a frozen
specification plus a seed-blind configuration-equality check, not on a single
revision. Ten of the fifteen cells predate the fields recording GPU identity and
carry nulls, which we did not backfill---a provenance field invented after the
fact is worse than an absent one. Every table and figure in this paper is
generated from the run outputs by scripts rather than transcribed; no number was
copied by hand.
