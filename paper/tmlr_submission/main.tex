\begin{abstract}
Conditional computation chooses either \emph{which} parameters process a token
---sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE)---or \emph{how much} computation it receives
---adaptive-depth weight-tied recursion (MoR). Composing them should inherit
both. We test whether it pays: one engine, three configurations, parameters
matched to 0.069\% ($\approx$5.58M on language), bit-identical inputs, five
seeds, exact randomisation tests under a pre-registered correction, on
WikiText-103 and on an arithmetic task built to exercise the composition.

We call the composition \emph{Mixture of Recursive Experts} (MoRE): a token is
routed once, from its post-embedding state, to one of $E$ independent expert
FFNs, and that expert's weights---never another's---are reused for an adaptive
number of recursion steps under learned halting. On the language task all three
arms sit on the quality/compute trade-off, and the composition buys the most
quality per unit of compute. It improves on MoE by $7.9\%$ validation loss at
$2.19\times$ MoE's inference FLOPs, where recursion alone needs $8.87\times$.
Excluding the output projection all three share, those become $17\%$ of
recursion's compute and $4.2\times$ the quality per unit compute. Out of domain
the composition is stronger still, reaching lower loss
than recursion alone at under a fifth of its compute; that comparison is
exploratory and carries no significance verdict.

On the language task it is not lowest-loss. Recursion alone leads by $0.1216$
nats, as extreme as a five-seed design admits and weaker than significance; we
attribute it to recursion activating six times more feed-forward parameters per
token at matched total parameters, which is its design rather than a confound.
Adding routing improves neither in-domain quality nor routing quality over MoE
on that task. Most transferably: a mean step count and an exit histogram cannot
establish input-dependent depth, since a uniform policy forced to the learned
mean reproduces both. Against that control on language, recursion's learned
policy is worth $1.471$ nats and the composition's $0.060$; we do not treat that
ratio as a verdict, its denominator being small.

On the arithmetic task, at its fixed three-epoch budget, the ordering reverses.
There the composition is the lowest-loss arm: $0.190$ nats against $0.245$ for
recursion alone and $0.430$ for MoE, lower in four of five seeds and with a
$4.8\times$ tighter spread than recursion. Routing is what stabilises recursion, and a fixed depth recovers most
of what the learned policy buys ($0.095$ nats against the best uniform depth),
so the halting head is input-dependent but not load-bearing.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Broader Impact Statement}

This work trains 5.6M-parameter language models on WikiText-103, a public corpus
derived from Wikipedia, and evaluates them on public benchmarks. No human
subjects, annotation, or personally identifying data are involved, and the models
are far below the scale at which generative misuse is a practical concern. The
realistic effect of the work runs through other researchers' decisions rather
than through deployment: we report a negative result about an architecture
combination, and a diagnostic---scoring any learned depth policy against a
depth-matched uniform policy before describing it as adaptive---that should save
compute otherwise spent on a mechanism that is not paying for itself. The
asymmetric risk is worth naming: a null at 5.6M parameters could discourage work
that would succeed at scale, which is why \S\ref{sec:limitations} states the
scale objection as a falsifiable prediction rather than a hedge. The corpus
inherits the biases of Wikipedia, which we did not measure and which bear on any
downstream use of these checkpoints.
