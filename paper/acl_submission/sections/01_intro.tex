\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Conditional computation is motivated by one accounting argument---most tokens do
not need the whole model---made in two places that answer different questions.
Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) layers
\citep{jacobs1991moe,shazeer2017moe,fedus2022switch} decide \emph{which}
computation module processes a token; recursive weight-tied architectures with
learned halting \citep{graves2016act,dehghani2019ut,banino2021pondernet} decide
\emph{how much} computation it receives, reusing one block for a variable number
of steps. The decisions are over different objects, so they are orthogonal in
principle and composing them is an obvious thing to try: route a token to a
specialised expert, then reuse \emph{that expert's} weights for as many steps as
it appears to need. The composition inherits an efficiency argument from each
side. What it does not inherit is evidence---whether it beats either component at
a matched budget has not, to our knowledge, been isolated.

We call this composition \emph{Mixture of Recursive Experts} (MoRE): a token is
routed once, from its post-embedding state, to one of $E$ independent expert
FFNs, and that expert's weights---never another's---are reused for an adaptive
number of recursion steps under learned halting.

We isolate it. One training engine implements three configurations differing only
in the fields their definitions require: routing without recursion (MoE, six
experts on the language task, depth 1), recursion without routing (MoR, one
shared block, depth up to 7), and the composition (MoRE, six experts, depth up to
7). All three sit at
$\approx$5.58M parameters, share a bit-identical input representation, and train
on the same fixed split of WikiText-103 \citep{merity2017wikitext} with the same
optimiser budget, so a difference between arms cannot be a difference in the
harness, the data, or the parameter count. A second task---arithmetic with seven
operation groups, its own manifest and a matched budget within that task---uses
the same engine and is reported in \S\ref{sec:arithmetic}; it is built so that
routing and recursion both have something to do.

\paragraph{What we find.} The composition occupies the compute-efficient point of
the three, and the honest accounting of what it does and does not buy is the
paper.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1.5pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{On the language task, all three arms sit on the quality/compute
  frontier, and the composition buys the most quality per unit of compute.} MoRE
  improves on MoE by
  $7.9\%$ validation loss ($3.948 \rightarrow 3.637$ nats/token) at $2.19\times$
  MoE's inference FLOPs where MoR needs $8.87\times$: $72\%$ of MoR's gain for
  $25\%$ of its compute, roughly $3\times$ the quality per unit compute. Out of
  domain \citep{gao2020pile} the ratio becomes outright \emph{dominance}---MoRE
  reaches 7.573 nats against MoR's 7.802 at a quarter of the compute, so MoR is
  Pareto-dominated---which is the one respect in which composing the mechanisms
  does something neither does alone.

\item \textbf{But on the language task recursion alone remains the lowest-loss
  arm.} MoR reaches 3.515 against MoRE's 3.637: the composition trades $0.1216$
  nats, significant at our resolution floor, for a $4\times$ inference-FLOP
  saving. These are two axes and we never substitute one for the other. On that
  task, adding routing improves neither in-domain quality over MoR nor routing
  quality over MoE.

\item \textbf{Recursion's adaptive depth is real on the language task; the
  composition's barely survives there.} Against a \emph{depth-matched} uniform
  policy---forced to the learned policy's own mean depth, so total compute is
  fixed---MoR's halting buys 1.47 nats and MoRE's 0.06, a factor of 24. MoRE's
  halting has collapsed to running to budget on $97.3\%$ of tokens.

\item \textbf{On an arithmetic task built to exercise the composition, it is
  the lowest-loss arm.} Seven operation groups, no group label in the input, and
  a difficulty axis written into the input: MoRE reaches $0.190$ nats against
  $0.245$ for recursion alone and $0.430$ for MoE, lower in four of five seeds
  and with a $4.8\times$ tighter spread than recursion. Routing is what
  stabilises recursion; the halting head is input-dependent but replaceable by a
  fixed depth ($0.095$ nats against the best uniform depth).

\item \textbf{A diagnostic that generalises.} A mean step count and an exit
  histogram---what this literature almost always reports---do \emph{not} establish
  input-dependent depth, since a policy ignoring its input reproduces both.
  Scoring against a depth-matched uniform policy does, costs one inference pass,
  and is what separates our two arms, which are otherwise indistinguishable.

\item \textbf{The two mechanisms compete for one budget, and two rival
  explanations are excluded.} A sweep that recovers adaptive depth by raising the
  ponder penalty destroys expert differentiation, while the arm that preserves
  differentiation saturates depth. Capacity dilution is ruled out
  arithmetically---tokens per parameter are identical by construction---and
  subspace trapping is falsified three ways (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}).

\end{itemize}

We report this as a trade-off measured at this scale, not a verdict on either
mechanism in general. The contribution is the decomposition: because MoE
\emph{is} MoRE with the depth budget set to one and all three arms are
parameter-matched from one codebase within each task, each contrast varies one
thing.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Conditional mixtures of experts date to \citet{jacobs1991moe}; the sparsely-gated
form is due to \citet{shazeer2017moe}, with the Top-1 dispatch and load-balancing
machinery of GShard \citep{lepikhin2021gshard}, Switch Transformer
\citep{fedus2022switch} and ST-MoE \citep{zoph2022stmoe}---whose standard
justification, more parameters at constant FLOPs, is unavailable here since we
pin all three arms to one parameter count. On the depth axis, Adaptive
Computation Time \citep{graves2016act} supplies the halting unit we use,
Universal Transformers \citep{dehghani2019ut} applied it to a weight-tied block,
PonderNet \citep{banino2021pondernet} reformulated the halting distribution to
keep a gradient near saturation, and early-exit variants place the decision at
the layer or token level \citep{elbayad2020depthadaptive,schuster2022calm,raposo2024mod}.
That literature reports mean step counts and exit histograms almost universally,
which \S\ref{sec:halting} argues cannot test input-dependence. Closest to us,
\citet{bae2025mor} and \citet{raposo2024mod} route \emph{to depths} over shared
weights; MoRE instead routes among independent expert FFNs whose weights the
recursion then reuses, and we know of no matched-budget comparison of such a
composition against each of its components---the gap we fill. We claim no novelty
for the combination itself. Minimal-pair and likelihood probes
\citep{warstadt2020blimp,ivanova2024ewok} score a \emph{relative} preference
within a matched pair, so they degrade gracefully with scale, which is what makes
them usable at 5.6M parameters.
