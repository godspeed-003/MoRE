\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

At 5.6M parameters on WikiText-103, composing sparse expert routing with
adaptive recursion buys efficiency and robustness rather than peak quality. All
three arms lie on the quality/compute frontier; MoRE buys the most quality per
unit of compute ($72\%$ of recursion's gain for $25\%$ of its compute) and out of
domain \emph{dominates} recursion alone, reaching lower loss at a quarter of the
compute---the one respect in which composing the mechanisms does something
neither does alone. What it does not buy is in-domain quality on the language
task: recursion alone leads by 0.1216 nats, significant at our resolution floor.
The language mechanism is not the obvious one---routing does not dilute the
update budget, and subspace trapping fails three ways---but a competition for one
per-token budget, in which the arm preserving specialisation saturates its depth.

The arithmetic task, built so that routing and recursion both have something to
do, reverses the ordering: there the composition is the lowest-loss arm and the
most stable one, and a fixed depth recovers most of what its learned policy buys.
The general lesson is that neither mechanism's value transfers across settings by
assumption: what a composition buys is a property of the task, and the controls
that establish it cost one inference pass. We release the engine, the evaluation
battery, the forced-depth control, and the arithmetic task generator, and it is
the control we would most like to see reused, because it is what separates two
arms that are indistinguishable by mean-and-histogram.

\paragraph{Future work.} Two extensions follow from the implementation as it
stands. The engine applies one dense block per recursion step; stacking several
distinct blocks and backpropagating through the whole stack would separate a
property of weight-tied depth from a property of the single-block budget, and
would let parameter depth and recursion depth vary independently within an
expert. Second, every expert currently sees the raw token embedding, so each must
model English and its nuances before it can exercise its specialisation. An
input-decoding stage---layers shared by all experts that encode the token into a
form suited to the task---would let the expert FFNs specialise on the
routed computation rather than on decoding, and would test whether the routing
gains observed here are limited by representational capacity at the input.
