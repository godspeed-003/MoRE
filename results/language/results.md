Matched to 0.069%. MoR's `ffn_mult = 24` is not a tuned value: it is 4x6, equalising
*total* FFN hidden width against MoRE's six experts. The residual gap is the router
plus six halt heads plus FFN biases, and it runs AGAINST the headline ordering --
MoR wins with fewer parameters, so capacity cannot explain the result.

**Active** parameters per token differ, and this is the one confound the ordering
does not remove: top-1 routing dispatches one of six 4-wide experts (524,288 FFN
params) in MoE and MoRE, while MoR's single 24-wide block activates 3,145,728 --
exactly 6x. MoR is the lowest-loss arm. The honest reading is that MoR buys its
quality with 6x the active feed-forward work at matched total parameters.

**This is the study's most uncomfortable result and it is stated rather than

6.73, with 97.3% and 81.2% of tokens halting at the maximum budget.

difficulty, and cannot at this ceiling.

## 9. Ablations and auxiliary evaluations

**Forced-depth sweep (inference-time).** Each canonical checkpoint re-run with
every token forced to exactly d steps. The readout necessarily changes with it: under
a forced schedule the ACT-weighted mixture degenerates to the final hidden state,
so the sweep measures reliance on the learned policy AND the readout together, and
the gap cannot be attributed to input-dependent depth alone. MoR's learned policy is
worth 1.471 nats over a depth-matched forced d=7; MoRE's is worth 0.060 nats. The
ratio of those two is 24x, and its denominator is small and carries no interval, so
it is reported as two numbers and not as a verdict.

**Not run: a depth-MATCHED-TRAINED control.** The pre-registered fixed-depth-trained
arm was never run, so ACT's contribution is not isolated by the strongest available
test. Its Holm slot is deliberately left consumed and unfilled.

**Not run: a parameter-matched MoR.** A MoR narrowed to MoRE's active width at the
same depth would separate 'composition' from 'sparse activation'. The parameter-
matched comparison that IS in `updated_objective.md`'s required ablations is absent.

**Update-state cosine (exploratory, uncorrected).** Mean cosine between a step's
update and the state it updates from, per recursion step:

| arm | cos(dh, h) per step | velocity per step |
|---|---|---|
| MOR | -0.26 -0.17 -0.14 -0.15 -0.18 -0.24 | 11.06 7.80 6.61 6.10 5.92 5.71 |
| MORE | -0.27 -0.12 -0.08 -0.05 -0.06 -0.07 | 9.09 5.48 4.74 4.24 3.82 3.49 |

The criterion is TWO-SIDED: +1 is state amplification, -1 is contraction toward
the origin, and only 0 marks genuinely new directions. Every value is negative
but small, so neither arm rescales its state. The arms are NOT equivalent,
though: MoRE's cosine decays toward orthogonality with depth while MoR's
persists and re-deepens at the final step, at roughly 3x MoRE's magnitude over the
deepest steps. Velocity decays in both, which is consistent with settling AND
with fixed-point trapping and therefore discriminates neither.

**Subspace-trapping probe (exploratory, uncorrected).** Final-state participation
ratio and between-expert separation:

| arm | global participation ratio | mean per-expert PR | final-state between-expert cosine |
|---|---|---|---|
| MOE | 41.5 | 30.6 | 0.630 |
| MOR | 70.3 | 70.3 | N/A |
| MORE | 57.3 | 44.4 | 0.916 |

Recursion RAISES the participation ratio, so a collapse onto a low-dimensional
subspace is not what happens. NOTE the final-state between-expert cosine (0.916 for
MoRE) is a DIFFERENT quantity from the expert-weight cosine in section 7 (0.0791),
and the two are not the same measurement. The probe is exploratory and its
comparisons are uncorrected.

## 10. Compute accounting

| arm | inference FLOPs/token | mean depth | x MoE | throughput (items/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MOE | 5,249,536 | 1.000 | 1.00x | 327.0 |
| MOR | 46,543,763 | 6.730 | 8.87x | 55.1 |
| MORE | 11,497,081 | 6.955 | 2.19x | 96.3 |

FLOPs come from a real forward pass counted with `torch.utils.flop_counter`
(linearity residual 0.0), not from an analytic formula. **All three arms share
one output projection worth 4,194,304 FLOPs/token -- 79.9% of MoE's total.** It is
identical across arms, so it cancels in a ratio OF ratios but not in a ratio
AGAINST MoE, whose total is small enough for a constant to dominate it. Both
accountings are reported because they disagree in direction: excluding the
constant, MoRE looks dearer than MoE (6.92x) and cheaper than MoR (17.2% of MoR's
per-token compute, with its quality-per-FLOPs advantage growing from 2.9x to
4.2x in domain and 5.7x to 8.1x out of domain). The composition wins the compute
comparison under either accounting, and by more under the stricter one.

**Throughput is not FLOPs.** MoRE is often the slowest in wall-clock despite doing
far less arithmetic than MoR, because per-token expert dispatch is latency-bound.
FLOPs is the compute measure; throughput is reported so the latency cost is visible.

## 11. Negative results and failure cases

- **Adaptive depth is not exercised at this scale** (section 8). The architecture
  supports it; the runs do not show it.
- **MoRE does not improve in-domain quality over MoR** (+0.1216 nats, at the p-floor).
- **MoRE does not improve routing quality over MoE** -- the arm with the same
  parameter count and no recursion.
- **Out of domain every arm is far from the uniform-loss ceiling of 9.011 nats**
  (7.2-7.9), i.e. the task is barely learned out of domain, so the ordering there
  is weak evidence. It is exploratory and carries no verdict.
- **The pre-registered depth-difficulty correlation is near zero** and uninformative
  for MoRE, whose depth barely varies.
- **The fixed-depth-trained control and the parameter-matched MoR were not run.**

## 14. Paper-safe claims

Each is supported by a number in this file or in a named artifact.

1. At matched parameters, MoRE buys the most quality per unit of inference compute
   of the three arms, on every metric measured (section 10).
2. Recursion is the mechanism that lowers loss at this scale: MoR beats MoE by
   0.4329 nats at the p-floor (section 5).
3. MoRE's in-domain quality does not exceed MoR's, and its routing quality does not
   exceed MoE's (sections 5, 7).
4. The router's agreement with a POS prior beats its own shuffled permutation
   control (section 7).
5. The recursion does not rescale the state: the update-state cosine is negative
   but far from -1 (section 9).

## 15. Explicitly unsupported claims

Each is a claim a reader might make from this data that the data does NOT license.

1. **That the model learns adaptive depth.** It does not at this scale; depth sits
   at the ceiling (section 8).
2. **That MoRE beats MoR.** It does not, in domain (section 5).
3. **That the composition adds something beyond sparse activation.** Untested: the
   parameter-matched MoR was never run (section 9).
4. **That ACT's learned policy is what makes recursion work.** Untested: the
   depth-matched-TRAINED control was never run (section 9).
5. **That the out-of-domain ordering is a robustness result.** It is exploratory,
   uncorrected, on a task every arm barely learns (section 11).
6. **That 'significant' is the right word.** Every comparison is at the exact test's
   resolution floor (section 5).
7. **That low expert cosine proves orthogonality.** It is consistent with
   differentiated parameterisations and nothing stronger (section 7).
8. **That the OOD set was fixed before the first look at it.** It was not; the
   capped re-scoring rule was fixed before scoring, and both sets are reported.

## 16. Final assessment

Outcome **B** -- a publishable exploratory result. MoRE is not the lowest-loss
architecture, but it delivers a stable and interpretable combination: the quality of
recursion at a fraction of the compute, with specialised routing that beats its own
permutation control, and with the mechanism's limits stated rather than argued past.
The honest headline is EFFICIENCY, and the honest limitation is that adaptive depth
is not demonstrated at this scale.
